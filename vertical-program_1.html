Why This Will Work for You

01
You're strong, but with a depth gap. At 145 kg × 3-4 partial reps (~1.8× bodyweight), you have a solid strength base — but it's partial range. This program adds full-depth squat work to unlock that strength where it matters (bottom of the jump), while focusing most of your energy on explosive power and plyometrics where the biggest gains are.
02
Untapped plyometric potential. You have almost zero plyometric training history. This means your stretch-shortening cycle (the elastic "spring" in your tendons) is undertrained. Beginners to plyometrics often see rapid early gains — 5-10 cm in the first 6 weeks is common.
03
Technique is free centimeters. Your arm swing timing, penultimate step mechanics, and approach angle all contribute to jump height. Cleaning these up can add 3-5 cm with zero physical adaptation needed.

Day D — Home: Plyo + Technique
Sunday • Home Session
~35 min ▼

Approach Jump Practice (15 min)

1-Step Approach Jumps
Approach Jump Technique Technique
5 × 3 jumps — Rest 2 min
One strong penultimate step into a max vertical jump. Focus: plant heel first on the last step to convert horizontal speed into vertical lift. Arms swing UP hard. Film yourself to check form.
2-Step Approach Jumps
2-Step Approach Technique
5 × 3 jumps — Rest 2 min
Left-right-jump (or right-left-jump). Last two steps should be quick and aggressive. The second-to-last step (penultimate) is longer, the last step is shorter and faster. Think "long-short-UP".

Reactive Plyometrics (10 min)

Continuous Vertical Jumps
Continuous Jumps Plyo
3 × 5 reps — Rest 2 min
Jump, land, immediately jump again. Minimal ground contact time. You're training the stretch-shortening cycle — the faster you reverse direction, the higher you'll jump.
Pogo Hops (bilateral)
Pogo Hop Form Plyo
3 × 15 reps — Rest 60 sec
Both feet, stiff ankles, quick bounce. Single-leg pogos produce ~5× bodyweight force — too intense for Phase 1. Build the spring with bilateral work first. Single-leg comes in Phase 2.

Penultimate Step Drill (5 min)

Penultimate Step Walk-Throughs
Penultimate Step Drill Technique
8–10 slow walk-through reps
This is the #1 technique for free centimeters. Walk through the last 3 steps of your approach in slow motion: long penultimate step (sit into it, ~90° knee bend, heel contacts first) → short fast final step → explode UP. Your penultimate step should be your LONGEST step. Practice until the rhythm feels automatic before adding speed. Film yourself from the side.

Week 4 Deload: Do the same sessions but cut sets in half (e.g., 4 sets → 2 sets). Same intensity, half the volume. Your body adapts during rest, not during training.

The Non-Negotiables

01
Every rep is max effort. Plyometrics at 70% effort are just cardio. If you're not trying to jump as high as humanly possible on every rep, you're wasting your time. Do fewer reps with full intent rather than more reps while tired.
02
Rest fully between sets. This is not HIIT. Power training requires complete nervous system recovery. If you're breathing hard, wait longer. 2-3 minutes minimum between plyometric sets.
03
Stop the set when speed drops. If your 4th rep feels slower than your 1st, the set is over. End it. More slow reps don't make you faster — they make you better at being slow.
04
Sleep 7-8+ hours. Your nervous system adapts during deep sleep. Training 5 days a week on 5 hours of sleep will burn you out and stall progress. This is as important as the training itself.
05
Don't add extra work. No morning split squats, no extra basketball runs on rest days, no "just a quick leg session." The program is designed to maximize stimulus with minimum fatigue. Adding more breaks it.
06
Track your jumps. Every Saturday, after your approach jumps, mark the wall or note your rim reach. Progress isn't always linear — you might plateau for 2 weeks then jump 3 cm overnight. The trend is what matters.

Technique Cues Cheat Sheet

Arm Swing
Arm Swing Tutorial
Arms go from behind your hips to overhead in one violent swing. Think about punching the ceiling. This alone can add 3-5 cm. Practice it standing still first — arms back, swing up, rise onto toes.
Penultimate Step
Penultimate Step Tutorial
The second-to-last step of your approach should be your LONGEST step. You're loading the spring. The last step is short and fast — it's the trigger, not the load. Pattern: long → short → UP.
Heel-to-Toe Plant
Heel-Toe Plant Tutorial
On your final step, your heel hits first, then rolls to the ball of your foot as you take off. This converts your horizontal run-up speed into vertical lift. If you plant flat-footed, you lose energy. If you're on your toes, you can't brake hard enough.
Hip Extension
Hip Extension Tutorial
At takeoff, your hips should fully extend — think about driving your hips THROUGH the ceiling. Many jumpers leave their hips behind. Full hip extension + arm swing = max height.

Nutrition Notes

At 72 kg and 179 cm, you're at a good weight-to-height ratio for jumping. Don't try to bulk up — every extra kilogram of non-functional mass is a kilogram you have to lift off the ground.

Protein
1.6–2.0 g per kg bodyweight (115–145 g/day). This supports tendon remodeling from the plyometric stress. Tendons adapt slower than muscles — protein helps.
Carbs Around Training
Explosive training runs on glycogen. Have a carb-rich meal 1-2 hours before training and refuel after. Don't train fasted for this program.
Creatine (optional)
5g daily. One of the very few supplements with solid evidence for improving power output. Cheap, safe, effective. Take it any time of day with water.

Red Flags — When to Pull Back

Sharp knee or Achilles pain
Dull soreness is normal. Sharp or pinpoint pain is not. If either knee or Achilles hurts sharply during jumps, take 3-4 days off plyometrics and do light cycling or walking only. If it persists, see a physio before returning.
Feeling "flat" for more than a week
If you feel heavy and sluggish for 5+ consecutive training days, take an unscheduled deload (3-4 days of rest). Overreaching kills vertical gains faster than anything.
Shin splints
Common when starting plyometrics. Train on softer surfaces (grass, rubber gym floor) instead of concrete. If it develops, reduce pogo hop volume first.
